What the colours meanhide
Hue = which sector (9)
Brightness & form = what kind of quantity
Solid, bright — fractional attributed effort. The honest count: every commit split across sectors, weights summing to 1.
Ghost, faint — secondary reach beyond that effort. Where the ghost runs well past the solid, the old single-sector model was throwing that difference away.
Quadrant view — each limb takes a representative hue from its member sectors: copy, workflows, seo, coding.
Quadrant view — one box per quarter
One box = one quarter. Eight boxes, stacked bottom→top up the time axis and threaded on the stem. Its FOOTPRINT AREA is that quarter's total attributed effort (model A weights, commits only) — which equals its commit count, because the weights sum to 1 per commit: 3 / 47 / 85 / 124 / 368 / 255 / 477 / 390. Height is constant, because the vertical axis is time.
Four quadrants = the four limbs. The box is cut into four wedges, each on its own limb's bearing — the same direction that limb points in the 9-sector view. A wedge's AREA is that limb's share of the quarter (radius ∝ √share, so area is exactly proportional). The bright arc cap marks the dominant limb, because the area encoding is honest but compresses near-ties.
Halo ring — the ghost. Its THICKNESS is that quarter's ghost-to-effort ratio: secondary reach per unit of captured effort. A ratio is positive by construction, so unlike the old fin-and-tail this never needs flooring (0%, against 68% before) and is comparable across quarters. Still a different unit from the box — do not subtract.
Grey stub — a measured zero: that limb did nothing that quarter. A deliberately flat, blunt wedge at the stem — a different shape family from a small wedge, so “nothing” cannot be mistaken for “a little”.
Other marks
Ground arcs — each sector's bearing swept to its parent limb's bearing, in the limb's hue. Under centroid bearings they converge exactly; under even 90° they land short or long, and that residual is what even spacing costs.
Dark red band — 2025-02, a month with no activity at all. A real pinch in the solid, never interpolated over.
Brown core — a drawn floor, not a measurement. Sectors at zero rest on it so the object stays readable.
t Badge colour = how a repo's weights were derived: topics from evidence, authored asserted by hand, single today's sector.
Gini Amber numbers are inequality figures, not counts.
Angle around the object is which sector — an ordered category, not a compass direction. Height is time.
The ruleset — where the weights come from

weight(sector) = share of that repo's GitHub topics mapping to it. Systematic, auditable, and an extension of the topic-map already in tree.config.yml — not a parallel mechanism. Topics captured 2026-08-01 and baked in; no API call at runtime.

Worked example — flickr-to-instagram-automation, 16 topics: 3 image (flickr-photos, gpt-4-vision, vision-api), 9 workflow, 4 agent → .19 / .56 / .25. That single repo moves 208 commits off a pure workflow reading.

Gini is inequality across the 9 sectors: 0 = every sector equal, 1 = one sector has everything. Lower means the tree grows in more directions, which is the whole reason to do this.
Solid and ghost are different units. The solid is fractional effort (sums to 1749); the ghost is a count of secondary appearances, normalised to its own maximum. The gap reads as “more reach than captured effort” — do not subtract one from the other.
Model B's total is a different unit. 3405 axis-hits from 1749 commits — the shape is fine, the sums are not comparable to A, C or today.
2025-02 is missing from the log entirely: a real pinch in all three solids, never interpolated.
9 sectors. The 8 from radial-envelope plus agents; no-code and low-code stay dropped (unobservable in a commit log by construction). generator/taxonomy.mjs is untouched.
Bearings. The nine sectors are respaced to an even 40°. The four quadrants are no longer hard-coded to 45/135/225/315 — they now follow their members, so the two objects point the same way. Azimuth means “which sector”, not a compass direction.
The boxes are quarterly; the envelope is monthly. They are the same weights re-bucketed, not a second measurement — but they are not interchangeable at the same fidelity, and the quarterly view cannot show the 2025-02 pinch at all.
One box per quarter; its area is the quantity. Eight boxes, one per period, threaded on the stem. Footprint area = that quarter's total attributed effort; the four wedges are the four limbs, each on its own bearing, each wedge's area its share of the quarter (radius ∝ √share). Height is constant because the vertical axis is time. The halo thickness is the ghost-to-effort ratio — a different unit from the box, so do not subtract. The previous four-fins-per-quarter build is still available at ?qstyle=collars.
Area is read less precisely than length. That is the honest cost of one box per quarter: the collars encoded a fin's length from a shared origin, which the eye reads better than area. The trade was deliberate — one object per period matches the summary's altitude, and a slab whose length varies while its width does not is a bar pretending to be a box — but read the boxes for rank and composition, and the table for ratios. The plan view (__topdown()) removes perspective distortion and is where any size comparison should actually be made.
